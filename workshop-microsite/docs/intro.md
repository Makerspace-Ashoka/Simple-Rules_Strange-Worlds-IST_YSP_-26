# Pixels That Think: Exploring Cellular Automata

Welcome to an exploration of complexity through simple rules! In this workshop, you'll discover how basic interactions between cells on a grid can create fascinating patterns, behaviors, and even computational systems.

## What Are Cellular Automata?

Cellular automata are mathematical systems where cells on a grid follow simple rules based on their neighbors' states. Despite their simplicity, they can generate remarkably complex behaviors—from fractal patterns to self-replicating structures.

:::tip Did you know?
Cellular automata were invented by mathematicians Stanisław Ulam and John von Neumann in the 1940s, but became widely known through John Conway's "Game of Life" in the 1970s.
:::

## Workshop Structure

This workshop follows a **discover → understand → create** approach:

1. **Exploration**: First, you'll observe patterns in both 1D and 2D cellular automata
2. **Analysis**: Next, you'll analyze the rules that create these patterns
3. **Implementation**: Finally, you'll code your own cellular automaton and experiment with rule modifications

## What You'll Learn

By the end of this workshop, you will:

- Recognize how simple rules can generate complex behaviors
- Understand the fundamental principles of cellular automata
- Implement Conway's Game of Life in code
- Create your own custom rules and patterns
- Connect these concepts to broader computational and natural systems

## Essential Concepts

Throughout the workshop, we'll explore these key ideas:

- **Emergence**: How complex behaviors arise from simple rules
- **Self-organization**: How order can appear spontaneously in systems
- **Computational universality**: How cellular automata can perform calculations
- **Pattern formation**: How patterns in nature might emerge from simple interactions
